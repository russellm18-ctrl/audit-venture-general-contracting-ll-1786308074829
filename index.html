// Findings

## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your homepage declares "A reliably better way to build" and positions Venture as a strategic development partner capable of delivering 31-story mixed-use high-rises. That is a premium claim aimed at sophisticated owners, developers, and institutional tenants. The forensic scan confirms 10 content images are present on the homepage — but every single one of them is invisible to search engines and screen readers because all 10 carry zero alt text. What that means at the brand level: you have invested in visual assets to carry the "Venture Difference" argument, and then stripped out every mechanism that would let those assets do work beyond the first human glance. A developer evaluating you against a competitor whose images are properly tagged is comparing a documented portfolio against silence. The trust cost is a perception gap between the scale of projects you can build and the apparent rigor with which you manage your own brand infrastructure.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your link audit is clean — 25 of 25 links resolve, which is not nothing. But the project carousel indexing tells a different story about narrative control. The scrape captures carousel position markers running 01/14 through 04/14, with the slider defaulting mid-sequence and project labels appearing out of order in the raw HTML. A high-value buyer — a developer comparing three GCs in an afternoon — does not wait for a carousel to cycle. They read what loads. If the featured sequence surfaces "Grata at Totem Lake" before "Skyglass" or "Sounders FC Center at Longacres," you are leading with affordable housing work on a page whose copy promises strategic partnership on complex, high-profile projects. The visual story fractures at exactly the moment a decision-maker is forming their first impression of your project tier. That is not a design preference issue — it is a sequencing failure that signals operational looseness to buyers trained to notice it.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your page title reads "Home Page - Venture" at 19 characters. Google allocates roughly 60 characters for titles in search results. You are forfeiting 41 characters of prime keyword and positioning real estate on every organic impression your homepage generates. You rank #1 for your own brand name, which means searchers already know you exist — the title's job at that moment is to confirm capability and prompt the click. "Home Page - Venture" confirms nothing. A competitor who titles their page "Seattle Commercial General Contractor | Mixed-Use & High-Rise" converts that same branded search into a capability signal before a user ever lands. Your og:image is present, which protects social shares from pulling a broken preview — that is the one infrastructure win here. But the 100% alt-text gap across all 10 content images means every project photo is a dead asset for image search, accessibility compliance, and the crawlers that feed Google's visual indexing. Ten images, zero tags, zero findability contribution from any of them. That is not a small omission — it is your entire homepage visual layer opted out of search.
